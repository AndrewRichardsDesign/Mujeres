Give to Gain

120,000+ participants

Causes

  1. An end to femicides and gender-based violence
  2. Justice for victims of violence
  3. Greater safety for women
  4. Recognition of disappearances and missing persons cases

Mexico 2026

Testimonial

"The night before the march, we gathered in a small kitchen in Coyoacan to paint our signs. My grandmother's hands, steady at eighty-two, traced the words 'Ni Una Menos' in purple ink. She told us about the women who walked these same streets decades ago, how their voices were swallowed by silence. Tomorrow, she said, the city will hear us."

1

A quiet morning before the gathering begins, anticipation filling the air.

0:00

We arrived at the Zocalo just after sunrise. The plaza was nearly empty, but you could feel it — the energy of what was coming. Women were quietly setting up banners, arranging flowers in the shapes of names we refuse to forget. A street vendor offered us coffee and said, "Today, the city belongs to you."

2

Voices unite in song, carrying stories passed through generations.

0:00

My abuela taught me the songs they sang in the seventies. I never understood them until I heard ten thousand women singing them in unison on Reforma. The melody carried decades of struggle, and every voice added another layer of defiance. We sang until our throats burned and then we sang louder.

3

Hands weaving together, creating something greater than the sum of its parts.

0:00

Strangers became sisters that afternoon. A woman I had never met took my hand as we crossed the intersection. She was trembling — not from fear, but from the overwhelming feeling of being seen. We held each other up, two pillars in a sea of purple, and I knew neither of us would ever march alone again.

4

Laughter echoes through the space, bonds strengthening with every moment.

0:00

Between the chants, there was laughter. Real, belly-deep laughter. My friend painted my face with glitter and we danced right there in the middle of the avenue. Joy is resistance too — they want us afraid, and we chose to be joyful. That laughter was the most radical act of the entire march.

5

A circle of trust, where every voice is heard and every story matters.

0:00

We formed a circle near the Angel of Independence. One by one, women spoke the names of those we have lost. With each name, someone lit a candle. By the time we finished, the ground was a constellation of flames. In that circle, silence spoke louder than any megaphone ever could.

6

Dancing under open skies, movement becomes the language of freedom.

0:00

A group of Indigenous women from Oaxaca began a traditional dance in the middle of the march. The drums echoed off the buildings and suddenly everyone stopped walking. We watched, mesmerized, as their feet told stories older than the city itself. Their dance was a reminder that this fight did not begin with us, and it will not end with us.

Testimonial

"When we returned home that evening, my feet aching and my voice nearly gone, I sat on the curb and cried. Not from exhaustion, but from the overwhelming feeling that something had shifted. My daughter, only seven, looked at me and said, 'Mama, will we march again next year?' I held her close and whispered, 'Every year, mija, until we don't have to anymore.'"
